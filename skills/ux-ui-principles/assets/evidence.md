### Method

Script per page: computed styles for body, first visible h1/h2/h3/p, first two
filled links/buttons; frequency of font-size (text-bearing nodes), font-family,
color, background, border-radius, box-shadow, gap, 1px borders; padding-top of
full-width blocks taller than 300px; centered max-width containers. Up to 3,000
visible nodes per page. Re-run before relying on any single value.

### Patterns that separate the strong pieces

1. **Imagery carries the page.** Most strong templates are built around one
   large photograph or generated visual, cropped into a rounded frame or run to
   the edge: soft natural light, macro textures (skin, water, glass, plaster),
   architecture, hands, products on plain grounds. A page without imagery looks
   like a wireframe whatever its type.
2. **Type laid over the picture.** Oversized display type sits on or against
   the image ("Skin, in good light.", "Architecture for everyday life.",
   a wordmark at poster size). The headline is part of the composition, not a
   box above it.
3. **Confident type pairs.** Large grotesk with a serif italic for emphasis;
   condensed display for agencies; tight tracking and 1.0 leading on display.
4. **Hero compositions beyond "text left, image right".** Image fan (3 to 5
   tilted cards), circular ring of thumbnails around the headline, image
   accordion (strips that expand), scroll-expanding media, centered headline
   over a product screenshot, full-bleed video or shader with a small caption.
5. **Backgrounds with depth.** Aurora and mesh gradients, grain, dither, dot
   fields, light beams, wireframe or particle shaders on dark grounds; on light
   grounds a soft radial glow. Used behind the hero only.
6. **Small proof details.** Announcement pill above the headline, avatar group
   with a star rating, logo cloud or marquee, big KPI cards ("99+"), sticker
   or doodle accents (arrows, scribbles) on playful brands.
7. **Pricing and cards with one emphasised tier.** A highlighted middle card
   (border, glow or inverted colour), monthly or yearly toggle, comparison
   table, usage slider; cards use a 1px border plus a very soft layered shadow.
8. **Galleries with depth.** Overlapping or rotated stacks, perspective walls,
   masonry with mixed crops, filmstrips, hover-expanding strips.
9. **Dashboards.** Light dashboards use a quiet sidebar, white cards on a
   tinted ground, area or bar charts in one hue and KPI cards with sparklines;
   dark and "fintech" dashboards use glass or gradient cards and a single
   saturated accent. Charts have soft gradients under lines.
10. **Interaction is part of the look.** Hover lifts, magnetic or liquid-glass
    buttons, scroll-linked reveals, number tickers. A still preview cannot show
    this; it needs motion specs ([motion](../references/motion.md)).

### What this means for our previews

- Our previews drew palette-coloured shapes. The gap to these pieces is
  imagery, depth (mesh, grain, glow, soft layered shadow), type laid over the
  picture and richer hero compositions, not more components.
- Added in `scripts/site_preview.js` (`--pages hero`, `home`): six hero
  compositions (fan, ring, accordion, overlay, product mock, centered), mesh
  and grain backgrounds, announcement pill, avatar group with rating and a
  placeholder logo cloud.
- Still missing: real photography (see [visual assets](../references/visual-assets.md)
  rung 1b), live shader or video backgrounds, and motion. Say so when
  presenting a preview.
- Conflicts: gradient, glow, glass and blur are banned by some other skills'
  house rules. Treat them as part of a chosen direction and record the override
  ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) "Working with other design skills").


## 21st.dev dashboard widget study

<!-- formerly assets/evidence.md -->

Observed 2026-11-05 on 21st.dev/community/components/s/dashboard (207 listed).
About 80 thumbnails were read by eye across the grid; none measured, nothing
copied. Community gallery: shows trends, not a standard.

### Widget types seen (most common first)

1. **Stat tile** — label, big number (tabular), delta pill (green/red, tiny arrow), a sparkline or "vs yesterday". Rows of 3–4.
2. **Area/line chart with an end dot** — soft gradient fill under a 2px line, last point marked with a glowing dot, light grid, month ticks only.
3. **Donut with centre total** — 2–5 parts, rounded caps, gap between arcs, total + caption inside; legend as dot + label + value + %.
4. **Data table** — hairline rows, avatar + two-line name cell, status dot-pill, progress bar column, row action "⋮", footer total row.
5. **Status/uptime strip** — 60–90 thin vertical bars per service (green, with amber/red incident bars), uptime % right-aligned, "all systems operational" pill on top.
6. **Ranked bars** — avatar, name, value, bar in palette colours, sorted; "bar race" playback control.
7. **Ring meters row** — 3–4 circular gauges with % inside, caption under; dark panel.
8. **Resource cards** — host name, status chip, CPU/memory/disk as thin labelled bars with threshold colour.
9. **Big number hero** — one KPI at display size over a faint area chart (orders, balance).
10. **Calendar / task list / activity feed** — dense month grid with event chips; checklist with due-date chips (Today / Yesterday / 1 week).
11. **Bento KPI grid** — mixed tile sizes, one tile dark or tinted to anchor the eye.
12. **Odd ones** — pixel/weather widget, isometric 3D chart, crypto cards each with own sparkline, brutalist bordered analytics. Novelty, rarely reusable.

### What makes them look finished

- One number is much larger than everything else in the card; labels are small and muted.
- Delta pills use a tinted background, not just coloured text.
- Charts drop axes and gridlines to a whisper; the data mark carries the colour.
- Cards use a large radius and one soft shadow; dark ones use a 1px lighter border, not shadow.
- A tile or panel in an accent or dark tone anchors a light page.
- Tables breathe: 48–56px rows, no vertical rules.
- Mono type for numbers and IDs in technical dashboards (infra, logs, API).
- Empty space inside cards is allowed; cards are not stretched to fill.

### What looks weak (seen often)

- Eight identical white tiles with a line chart each.
- Tiny unreadable labels in thumbnails (under 10px) — copy the layout, not the size.
- Donuts with 6+ segments or no legend.
- Dark panels with grey-on-black captions under 4.5:1.

### Use in this skill

Rules went into `references/back-office.md` ("Widget recipes") and
`references/data-visualization.md` (uptime strip, end-dot chart, ring meters).

Typical: display 72-106px, weight 400 for serif and 700-800 for sans, tracking -0.02 to -0.04em, section padding 96px or
more. This matches our token defaults (display up to about 84px, -0.03em, 96px), so the gap is not scale.

### What makes the best ones beautiful (observed, not assumed)

1. **One decisive move per page.** A full-bleed photograph with a giant wordmark (luxury real estate), a single "04" numeral at viewport scale (Swiss), a two-line serif headline with one italic word (warm paper). Everything else is quiet.
2. **A typographic voice, not a font choice.** Roman plus *italic emphasis* inside one headline, tiny uppercase mono labels, oversized numerals, serif for numbers that matter. The pairing carries the identity before color does.
3. **Editorial furniture.** Register strips (issue, volume, date, coordinates), vertical text rails, plate numbers, "Vol. 01 / No. 26", crop-mark corners, filed-under labels, marginalia. Tiny, mono, low contrast; it makes a page feel authored.
4. **Real imagery or none.** Where a photograph appears it carries the page; where there is none, type and rules do all the work. They avoid weak generic illustration.
5. **Restrained palette.** Ground, ink and one accent taken from the subject (the studied set used warm paper with terracotta, coral, hazard red or cobalt); dark dashboards add one highlight color for the single bar or number that matters. Warm cream + serif + terracotta is also the most common generated look ([design quality](../references/design-quality.md) §2): use it only when the subject is paper or craft, and then change at least two of ground, face and accent from the obvious choice.
6. **Dashboards with a point of view.** One editorial example sets large serif numerals on tan with sparse labels and a single accent bar in a black bar chart; the dark social dashboard leads with a sentence ("shipped 9 posts this week") before the numbers.
7. **Objects with weight.** Pinned paper notes at a slight tilt with a soft shadow, a phone or device frame, a floating pill nav. Depth is used rarely and on purpose.
8. **Honest scale.** Product pages (docs, pricing, blog) are plain and crisp, not decorated; beauty comes from spacing and type, not effects.

### Where we differ

- Our previews draw art from the palette; the best references use a photograph or pure typography. See [visual assets](../references/visual-assets.md) for sourcing real pictures.
- We have the tokens and shapes; we lacked "furniture" and typographic-voice recipes. Added to [direction recipes](../references/direction-recipes.md).
- The library page ships 52 style-named DESIGN.md files, but many keep default placeholder palettes (the same blue and violet repeat in about half); only a few (Warm Editorial, Kami, Atelier, the brand-inspired ones) are specific enough to learn from.

### Design-system library page (the site's own design)

Giant two-line grotesque H1 ending in one lime dot; a wide-tracked uppercase eyebrow with a short leading rule; pill search
field with a `/` key hint; segmented "All / New (126)" control; a left category list with counts (Enterprise & SaaS 51,
Public services & education 38, UI libraries 36); cards that show a 4-swatch palette, a one-line description and a
"N-chapter guide" tag. Useful pattern: palette chips plus a one-sentence purpose on every card.


## Open Design template skills: patterns kept

<!-- formerly assets/evidence.md -->

Read 2026-11-05 from the OpenDesign plugin catalogue (`open-design.ai/plugins/templates/`, 265 entries: prototype 85,
slides 81, image 46, video 22, hyperframes 25, live artifact 5, audio 1). The detail pages only carry metadata, so the
rules were read from the `SKILL.md` of 51 web, dashboard, mobile, wireframe, document and utility templates in the public
repo (`nexu-io/open-design`, `plugins/_official/examples/`). Rules are paraphrased; nothing was copied. Slides, image,
video and audio templates were left out (outside this skill). Earlier, narrower review: `../layouts/opendesign.md`.

### What the template skills have in common

1. **Seed plus library, not a blank page.** Each reads a seed template, a section-layout library and a checklist before writing; tokens map to a handful of CSS variables and no new tokens are invented.
2. **A DESIGN.md is the input.** Several stop and ask when it is missing and treat its "agent prompt guide" as overriding the skill. (Our DESIGN.md follows the same spec: `references/design-memory.md`.)
3. **P0 / P1 / P2 checklists, "pass P0 or do not emit".** P0 examples: no raw hex outside the token block, accent at most twice per screen, no emoji as feature icons, no invented metrics, no filler copy, no remote image dependencies, mobile reflow works.
4. **State the plan in one sentence first.** The section list or layout choice is announced before any code, so it can be redirected cheaply.
5. **Honest placeholders.** `—` or a labelled placeholder where a number is unknown; plausible, specific sample data, never "Metric A".
6. **Pre-flight per aesthetic.** The three "taste" web prototypes (soft premium, editorial minimalism, industrial brutalist) each list hard rules, bans, required components, motion and a tick-box pre-flight.

### Observations about the catalogue

- Most "template" value is the checklist, not the markup: the strongest entries are the ones with a concrete pre-flight.
- Dashboards come in three flavors: static analytics, interactive (tabs, tooltips, export, dark toggle) and live (connector, refresh, diff). Only the live one defines data freshness states.
- Decks outnumber web templates; their craft rules (kicker/chrome vocabulary stays in one register) are the same as our direction-consistency rule.
- Source and attribution matter: several templates distill other open skills and say so; do the same when adapting.
